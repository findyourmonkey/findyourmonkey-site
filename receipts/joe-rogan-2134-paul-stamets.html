Official position

CDC describes pigs as a mixing vessel for influenza viruses and documents the 2009 H1N1 pandemic originated from swine viruses.

“Pigs can be infected by influenza viruses found in birds and other animals as well as people. Therefore, pigs represent a mixing vessel in which influenza viruses from different species can swap genes.”
Government / court record CDC H1N1 Flu | Origin of 2009 H1N1 Flu (Swine Flu)

Problem with it

H5N1 infection of pigs remains relatively rare (50 GISAID submissions by 2018) and sustained pig-to-human transmission of H5N1 has not been documented.

“the ability of H5Nx viruses to infect these species is still under investigation. As of October 2018, there have been 50 submissions of H5N1 swine infection uploaded to the GISAID”
Research / data A review of H5Nx avian influenza viruses - PMC

Conflicting reports

The disagreement: Whether H5N1 jumping to pigs would likely enable human infection because pig-infecting viruses can infect humans.

Why they disagree: Sources agree pigs are influenza mixing vessels with both avian/human receptors and historical precedent (2009 H1N1), but H5N1's ability to infect pigs and reassort into human-transmissible forms is still under investigation.

Evidence leans: MOSTLY_TRUE

Strong evidence that pigs are influenza mixing vessels with dual receptors and that swine-adapted viruses have jumped to humans (2009 H1N1). H5N1 has infected pigs and lab reassortants with human-adapted genes transmit in mammals. However, 'likely' overstates certainty for H5N1 specifically — natural reassortment yielding sustained human transmission hasn't occurred yet.

What would settle it: Documented H5N1 reassortment in pigs yielding human-transmissible variant, or sustained human outbreaks linked to pig-adapted H5N1.
