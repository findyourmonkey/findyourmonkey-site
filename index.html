Why ours are different

The machine checks its own work.

Anything can cut a video into pieces. These are the things that go wrong when nobody is watching — and what we do about each one.

It starts where the thought starts

  • Clips never open or close mid-sentence. Boundaries snap to real sentence ends, not to a stopwatch.
  • Length follows the moment. A joke that lands in 19 seconds isn't padded to fill a slot.
  • The title quotes words actually spoken in that clip — not a paraphrase of the episode.

Your sponsor never gets clipped

  • Ad reads are found and excluded before anything is cut.
  • Ads run in pods, so the whole block goes — not just the part with the promo code.
  • “Checked and clean” and “nobody looked” are tracked as different things. We never confuse the two.
  • Mid-roll break markers land in real, measured silence in the audio — not a transcript's best guess at where a sentence ends.

The captions match the audio

  • Every finished clip is transcribed again and compared to its own burned-in captions. Anything that drifted is rejected before you see it.
  • Captions sit clear of the buttons and handles the apps paint over your video.
  • Hosts swap fresh ads into a published file — we detect that and re-read the audio instead of trusting an old transcript.

Somebody is always on screen

  • The crop follows a real person, verified as a person — not a poster, a plant, or an elbow.
  • On multi-camera shows the framing is re-checked at every camera cut, not once per clip.
  • Audio-only show? Every frame is generated from your own words as you say them.
What that's worth: the failures above are the ones that make a clip unpostable — a punchline cut in half, captions a second behind the mouth, your sponsor's promo code in a Short. We found each one the hard way, on our own output, and the checks run on every clip whether or not anyone is looking.
